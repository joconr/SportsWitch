# SportsWitch privacy policy

*Last updated: October 31, 2026*

SportsWitch is a Chrome extension that recognises ad breaks in the sports streams you add to it, then switches
to another game or mutes the ad, and can fill the break with trivia, scores and music. This page explains, in
plain words, what it reads, what it keeps and what leaves your computer. The same summary is inside the
extension under **Settings › Privacy**.

## What SportsWitch reads and why

| What | Why | Where it goes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| The sound of the browser tabs you add with **Add This Tab** | To tell ads from the game | Analysed in Chrome on your computer, a second at a time. It is never recorded, saved as audio or sent anywhere. |
| The title, address and a small preview picture of those tabs | To name and show your games in the lineup | Kept only in Chrome on your computer while you use them. |
| What SportsWitch learns: fingerprints of ads it has confirmed, a short history of fingerprints of the game you are watching, and your "That's an ad" / "That's the game" corrections | To recognise ads faster on your streams | Kept only in Chrome on your computer. **Settings › Reset what SportsWitch learned** deletes it. Fingerprints are short number patterns; the sound can't be rebuilt from them. |
| Your settings and ad-break choices | To remember how you like it | Kept only in Chrome on your computer. |
| The detection log, only if you turn on **Save a detection log** | So you can study or retrain the detection | Numbers per second (no audio) and the titles of what was on, kept only in Chrome on your computer, up to 50 MB. You can download or delete it in Settings. |
| Whether you are signed in to espn.com, and your ESPN account ID (the SWID cookie), only if you set up fantasy football | To load **your own** ESPN Fantasy league and team | Sent only to ESPN, the same as when you open your league on espn.com. Your ESPN password never passes through SportsWitch. |
| Your Spotify tab on open.spotify.com, only if you turn on Spotify | To press play when a break starts and pause when the game is back, and show the song's name | SportsWitch reads only what the player shows (playing or paused, the song's title). It never sees your Spotify account or password. |

## What leaves your computer

Only the optional ad-break extras and update checks go online, and none of them carries information about you
beyond what any web page request does (such as your IP address):

* **Open Trivia DB** (opentdb.com) for trivia questions.
* **ESPN** (site.api.espn.com) for scores and standings, and, with fantasy football set up, ESPN's fantasy
  service with your own ESPN sign-in.
* **Radio Browser** (radio-browser.info) for the list of stations, and the station you pick for its music.
* **Spotify** plays in your own Spotify tab; SportsWitch sends nothing to Spotify itself.
* **Updates:** Chrome updates the Chrome Web Store copy. A copy loaded from a folder asks GitHub
  (api.github.com) once a day for SportsWitch's newest version. If you set up automatic updates, SportsWitch
  Updater, a small helper on your computer, downloads that version from GitHub and installs it. Neither sends
  anything about you.

## What SportsWitch never does

* It sends nothing about you to the developer. There are no accounts, ads, analytics or tracking.
* It does not sell, share or transfer your data to anyone, and uses it only for the purposes above.
* It does not record audio or video, and it does not remove or block ads; it switches or mutes.

## Changes

If an update changes what SportsWitch reads or where anything goes, its release notes and this page will say
so before it reaches you.
